Cabinet Refinishing · Lansdale, PA

A kitchen transformation without replacing the cabinets.

Cabinet refinishing can change the entire feel of a kitchen while keeping the layout and cabinetry you already have.

Cabinet refinishing can make a kitchen feel dramatically different without changing the layout or replacing cabinetry that still works well.

Cascade Painting provides cabinet refinishing for homeowners in Lansdale and nearby communities. Cabinet projects demand a different level of surface attention than ordinary wall painting because doors, drawer fronts and cabinet boxes are handled, cleaned and viewed at close range every day. The finish needs to look deliberate, and the preparation beneath it matters.

What a cabinet project can include

A typical cabinet scope may include removable doors, drawer fronts, exposed cabinet boxes, end panels, face frames and a contrasting or matching island. Hardware decisions, color changes and the existing cabinet finish can all affect how the project should be planned. We define the scope first so you know exactly which components are part of the refinishing work.

Preparation for adhesion and appearance

Kitchen cabinetry collects cooking residue, hand oils and household cleaners, so cleaning and surface preparation are important. Depending on the existing finish and condition, preparation may include degreasing, scuff sanding, spot repairs and an appropriate bonding or primer step before the finish system. The objective is a clean, consistent surface that is ready for the next coating rather than simply putting new color over whatever is there.

Doors, drawers and cabinet boxes

Doors and drawer fronts create most of the visual field in a kitchen, but the cabinet boxes and face frames determine whether the finished project looks complete when everything is reassembled. We treat those elements as one system and pay attention to edges, panel profiles and the places where painted components meet counters, walls and flooring.

Color changes and islands

Many cabinet projects use one color throughout, while others use a contrasting island to create a focal point. A large color shift—especially from a darker finish to a lighter one—can require a different preparation and coating approach than a small tonal change. We account for that when defining the scope.

Cabinet refinishing FAQs

What homeowners usually want to know first.

Is cabinet refinishing the same as replacing cabinets?

No. Refinishing updates the visible finish on cabinetry you are keeping. It is best suited to cabinets whose layout and basic condition still work for the homeowner.

Can the island be a different color?

Yes. A contrasting island is a common design direction and can be included as a separate color in the scope.

Do the cabinet doors come off?

Doors and removable components are generally handled separately so edges and profiles can be prepared and finished more consistently. The exact workflow is explained before the project begins.

How long before refinished cabinets can be used normally?

Dry time and full cure are not the same thing. Timing depends on the coating system and project conditions, so we provide project-specific guidance on gentle use and reassembly rather than giving one universal number.

Can every cabinet surface be refinished?

Not every material or existing finish is an equally good candidate. The estimate is where we evaluate the surface and determine whether refinishing is an appropriate approach.
